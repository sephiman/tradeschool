# Mass sentiment

Price is set by people, and people move in herds. When almost everyone feels the same way about the
market at the same time, that agreement itself becomes information — usually a warning. **Mass
sentiment** tools try to measure that mood. The best known in crypto is the **Fear & Greed index**,
and like every tool in this course it is useful only once you know exactly what it measures and what
it doesn't.

## What the Fear & Greed index actually is

The index (the widely used one is published by alternative.me) is a single number from **0 to 100**:
0 is "extreme fear", 100 is "extreme greed". It is a *composite* — it blends several inputs into one
score:

- **Volatility** — how far and how sharply price is swinging versus its recent average.
- **Market momentum and volume** — how strong and stretched recent buying is.
- **Social media** — the volume and tone of posts about crypto.
- **Dominance** — money shifting into or out of Bitcoin as a safe haven within crypto.
- **Trends** — search interest (spikes in "bitcoin" or in panic terms).

Notice something: **volatility and momentum are derived from price itself**, and together they are
about half the score. So the index is, to a large degree, a re-description of recent price action
dressed up as "emotion". That is not exactly a flaw — it is simply the thing you must remember about
it.

## What it measures — and what it doesn't

It measures **the tone of the recent past**: a fast rally with heavy volume and euphoric posts scores
greedy; a sharp drop with panic search spikes scores fearful. That is a real, useful summary of the
crowd's current mood.

It does **not** measure any of the things a beginner assumes it does. It does not know the future. It
does not tell you where price is going next, when a move will turn, or how much room is left. It is a
lagging, backward-looking read of *how it has felt lately* — not a forecast.

:::note{type=info}
Sentiment is a **context** indicator, not a signal generator. It answers "how crowded and one-sided
is the mood right now?" — never "should I enter here?". Treat the number as a description of the
weather, not an instruction.
:::

## Greed and euphoria: a zone for caution

When the index sits in **extreme greed**, the crowd is euphoric — confident, leveraged, and already
long. **Euphoria** is the emotional state where people buy because price is rising and cannot imagine
it falling. The contrarian logic is simple and honest: if almost everyone who wanted to buy has
already bought, there are few buyers left to push price higher, and a lot of nervous late longs who
will sell fast if it dips. Extreme greed does not mean "sell now". It means **the ground is crowded —
size down, tighten risk, stop chasing.**

## Fear and capitulation: a zone of opportunity

At the other end, **extreme fear** means the crowd is scared, and its endpoint is **capitulation** —
the moment holders give up and sell at a loss just to stop the pain. By the same logic, once the
people who were going to panic have already sold, selling pressure is largely spent, and price is
often near a low. That is why the deepest fear tends to appear near bottoms, not tops. Again: this is
a **zone of interest**, a place to look for setups, not a command to buy.

## Why it is a zone, not a trigger

Here is the mistake that costs people money. They see the index hit 10 and buy, or hit 90 and sell,
as if the number were an entry button. **Extremes can persist for weeks or months.** Greed stayed
extreme through entire bull runs while price kept climbing; fear stayed extreme through long grinding
bear markets while price kept bleeding. A contrarian who shorted the first "extreme greed" print, or
bought the first "extreme fear" print, got run over.

Sentiment marks a **contrarian zone** — a region where the odds and the risk/reward start to shift,
and where you should get *more careful* about the crowded side. It does not mark a moment. The
trigger to actually enter still has to come from your own tools: structure, a level, confirmation.
Sentiment tells you *which side is dangerous*; it never tells you *when*.

:::note{type=warning}
The classic trap: treating an extreme reading as a precise buy or sell signal. "Index at 8, back up
the truck" and "index at 92, short it" are how contrarians get liquidated early. Extremes tell you
the crowd is lopsided; they say nothing about timing. Wait for your own confirmation.
:::
