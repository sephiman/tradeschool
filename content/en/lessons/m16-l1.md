# Volatility cycles: compression, expansion and the squeeze

**m08-l2** gave you a single compression candle and **m15-l2** gave you a compression *pattern*. This
lesson takes the same idea one level up, to the whole market: **volatility clusters.** Quiet stretches
follow quiet stretches, violent ones follow violent ones, and the two take turns.

That is not a piece of folklore — it is one of the most robust statistical regularities in any traded
market, and it is why "it has been calm for three weeks" is genuine information while "it went up for
three weeks" mostly is not. The useful form of it is short: **a quiet market is not a safe market. It
is a market that has not moved yet.**

## Measuring it: Bollinger

A **Bollinger band** is the simplest possible measurement of "how dispersed has price been":

- a moving average of the close — the **basis**;
- plus and minus a number of **standard deviations** of the close (conventionally two) over the same
  window.

That is the whole construction. Two standard deviations of the closes, drawn as an envelope. When
closes have been scattered, the band is wide. When they have been bunched, it is narrow. **The width is
a read of realized volatility** — of what already happened — and that is all it is.

It is worth being blunt about what it is *not*, because this indicator attracts more mythology than
most. A touch of the upper band is not a sell signal. It means price is two standard deviations above
its own recent mean, which in a trending market is where price lives — the same error **m11** named for
an overbought RSI, wearing a different envelope.

## Measuring it: Keltner

A **Keltner channel** looks identical and is measured with a different ruler:

- the same kind of moving average in the middle;
- plus and minus a multiple of the **ATR** — the *average true range*, which is how far a typical bar
  travels from high to low, counting any gap between bars.

So the two envelopes answer two different questions:

| | what it measures |
|---|---|
| **Bollinger** | how far the **closes scatter** |
| **Keltner** | how far a **bar travels** |

Those come apart, and the way they come apart is the point. Picture a market where every bar swings a
long way and closes back near where it opened — busy inside each candle, going nowhere across them. The
ATR is high, so the Keltner channel is wide; the closes are all in a heap, so the Bollinger band is
narrow. Now picture a steady trend of small, orderly bars: the ATR is low and the closes are strung out all
the way up the move, so it is the other way round.

## The squeeze

That is what makes this comparison worth drawing at all:

> **When the Bollinger band sits entirely inside the Keltner channel, the market is in a squeeze.**

Read what that condition actually says. The closes have contracted *relative to how far the bars are
still travelling* — an unusual amount of compression, unusual in the specific sense that the two rulers
disagree. Ordinary quiet does not do it, because ordinary quiet shrinks both. The squeeze ends when the
Bollinger band pushes back outside, and that exit is the **expansion**.

The figure shows one cycle with both envelopes drawn. The window opens busy, at around **2,014**, with
the Bollinger band outside the Keltner channel. Then the closes pin themselves together while the bars
keep moving, the Bollinger band slides inside — the squeeze — and price sits near **1,902** going
nowhere. When the band pushes back out, the expansion carries it to **1,589**.

### The squeeze momentum indicator

The packaged version you will meet under names like *Squeeze Momentum* puts the two ideas in one pane:
a row of dots on a zero line showing whether the squeeze is on, and a **signed histogram** showing the
direction and force of the move.

The figure below adds that pane, on a second instance so you can see the reading hold twice. The window
opens near **10,361**, compresses around **10,551**, and the expansion carries it to **12,272**.

::figure{id=fig-m16-squeeze-momentum}

Notice what the pane adds: **nothing the price panel above it does not already contain.** The dots are
the Bollinger-inside-Keltner condition and the histogram is a smoothed measure of where price sits
relative to its own recent range. It is a convenience — one pane instead of four lines — and reading it
as an oracle is reading a repackaging of two averages as though it knew something. When the dots go
dim, the bands had already told you.

## The word "squeeze" means two unrelated things

**This is the disambiguation to get right, because the collision is total and the two mechanisms have
nothing to do with each other.**

1. **Volatility squeeze** (this lesson): a *measurement artefact* — the Bollinger band inside the
   Keltner channel. It describes how compressed price has been. There is no forced participant, no
   liquidation, nobody on the wrong side.
2. **Liquidity squeeze / short squeeze** (**m19-l2**): a *market event* — price rises into a pocket of
   short liquidations, those shorts are forcibly bought back, that buying pushes price higher, which
   liquidates more shorts. A feedback loop with real forced orders in it.

One is a statement about a chart. The other is a statement about who is being forced to trade and in
which direction. They share a word and nothing else. If somebody says "there's a squeeze setting up",
the two possible meanings differ on whether anything is happening at all.

## What compression actually tells you

The closing reading, and it is the same shape as **m19**'s open interest and funding:

> **Compression says an expansion is coming. It does not say which way.**

That is the honest limit. A squeeze is a statement about the *size* of the next move, not its
direction — exactly as rising open interest tells you positions are being added without saying by whom,
and funding tells you which side is paying without saying which side is right. The tool describes a
condition; the direction you read from the break, with the same body-close and participation discipline
**m08-l1** and **m14** ask for everywhere else.

And note the failure mode that follows directly. The expansion out of a squeeze is often violent, which
means a trade taken on the wrong side of it loses quickly. "The squeeze is about to fire" is not a
direction, so a position sized as though it were is a position sized on nothing — which is **m22** and
**m26**, again.
