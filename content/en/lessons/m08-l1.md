# Price structure

Price does not wander at random. It leaves a visible record of where buyers and sellers fought, and
that record — the **structure** — is the most honest chart-reading you can do, because it is the
price itself, not an indicator derived from it.

## Support and resistance are zones, not lines

A **support** is a price area where buying has repeatedly stepped in and stopped a fall. A
**resistance** is an area where selling has repeatedly capped a rise. The word to hold onto is *area*.

Beginners draw a single, pixel-thin line and then feel betrayed when price stops a little short of
it, or overshoots by a hair. It was never a line. It is a **zone** — a band a few candles wide —
because:

- Different traders placed their orders at slightly different prices.
- Wicks and closes tell different stories; the "level" is smeared across both.
- The more times an area is tested, the wider and more real the zone becomes, not the more precise.

Draw the zone from the **bodies and wicks of the swings that actually turned**, and expect price to
react *somewhere inside it*, not at one exact number.

:::note{type=warning}
The most common self-inflicted wound: drawing a level, watching price miss it by 0.3%, and
concluding "support failed." It didn't fail — you drew a line where the market drew a band. A zone
that gets poked and holds is doing its job.
:::

## Breakout vs fakeout

Sooner or later price leaves the range. The question is whether it *meant* it.

- A **genuine breakout** closes decisively **beyond** the level — a full candle body on the far side
  — and then **holds** there, ideally coming back to *retest* the old level as new support (or
  resistance) and bouncing away. Extra credit if the break happens on **higher volume**: it took
  real participation to get through.
- A **fakeout** (false break) pokes **beyond** the level — often on a long wick — and then closes
  **back inside** the range. The break is rejected. These trap the traders who bought the first
  candle past the line.

The single most useful discriminator: **wait for the close, not the poke.** A wick through a level is
a rumour; a body closing and holding beyond it is the news.

:::note{type=info}
Nobody can tell a breakout from a fakeout at the instant price crosses the line — not you, not
anyone. That is why patient traders wait for the close beyond, and often for the retest, accepting
they will miss the very first move in exchange for not being trapped by most fakeouts.
:::

## Market structure: HH/HL and LH/LL

Zoom out from the single level to the sequence of swings.

- An **uptrend** is a staircase of **higher highs (HH)** and **higher lows (HL)**: each rally exceeds
  the last peak, and each dip bottoms above the last dip.
- A **downtrend** is the mirror: **lower highs (LH)** and **lower lows (LL)**. Each bounce fails
  lower, each drop goes further.
- A **pullback** is the counter-move *within* a trend — the HL forming inside an uptrend. It is not a
  reversal; it is the trend catching its breath. The distinction only becomes clear once the next
  swing confirms it.

## Change of character

A trend stays intact as long as the staircase continues. It is broken by a **change of character
(CHoCH)**: the first swing that violates the pattern.

In an uptrend, that is the first time price makes a **lower low** — it fails to hold above the
previous HL. In a downtrend, it is the first **higher high**. The change of character is not a
promise that the trend has reversed; it is the first *evidence* that the old trend's rule has stopped
working. Often it precedes a longer transition or a range, not a clean flip.

:::note{type=warning}
"Change of character" gets sold as a magic reversal signal. It isn't. It's a broken pattern — a
reason to stop assuming the old trend and start watching for what replaces it. One lower low in an
uptrend can be a deep pullback that resumes; treat the CHoCH as a question, not an answer.
:::

## What to actually do

1. Mark the obvious zones first — the ones a stranger would circle. If you have to squint, it isn't a
   level.
2. Read the swing sequence: are you in HH/HL, in LH/LL, or in a messy range with neither?
3. At a level, wait for the **close**. Beyond and holding = breakout; beyond then back inside =
   fakeout.
4. Let the market confirm before you rename a pullback a reversal. The change of character is the
   earliest honest warning — no earlier.
