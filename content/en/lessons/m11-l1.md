# Oscillators: RSI and MACD

An **oscillator** is an indicator, derived from price, that moves within a bounded range and tries to
describe *momentum* — how fast and how one-sidedly price has been moving lately, not where it sits.
Two are everywhere in crypto: **RSI** and **MACD**. Both are genuinely useful once you know exactly
what they compute, and both are misread constantly by people who treat them as buy and sell buttons.

## What RSI actually computes

**RSI** (Relative Strength Index) compares the average size of recent *up* moves to the average size
of recent *down* moves, over a lookback period (the default is **14** bars), and squeezes the result
onto a **0–100** scale.

- If almost every recent bar closed up, average losses are near zero and RSI pins near **100**.
- If almost every recent bar closed down, average gains are near zero and RSI sits near **0**.
- When gains and losses are roughly balanced, RSI hovers around the middle, near **50**.

So RSI is a *relative* measure. It is not asking "is price high?" — it is asking "how lopsided have
the last 14 bars been?" The conventional bands are **above 70 = overbought** and **below 30 =
oversold**. But read on, because those two words are exactly where most beginners lose money.

## What MACD actually computes

**MACD** (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) is built from three pieces:

- The **MACD line**: the difference between a fast EMA (12) and a slow EMA (26) of price. When the
  fast average is above the slow one the line is positive — price is pulling away from its recent
  baseline.
- The **signal line**: a 9-period EMA of the MACD line — a smoothed, slower version of it.
- The **histogram**: the MACD line minus the signal line, drawn as bars. It is simply the *gap*
  between the two lines.

Read it as momentum, not as level. A **cross** of the MACD line up through its signal line (the
histogram flips from negative to positive) says short-term momentum is turning up relative to the
recent trend; a cross the other way says the opposite. A histogram that is *growing* means the move
is accelerating; a *shrinking* histogram means it is losing steam even while price is still rising.

:::note{type=info}
Both indicators are just arithmetic on past prices. They contain no information price does not already
carry — they *repackage* it into a momentum read. That is honestly useful for spotting acceleration
you might miss on the raw candles, and it is also why they can never "predict" anything on their own.
:::

## The overbought/oversold lie

Here is the single most expensive misunderstanding in this module. Beginners learn "RSI over 70 =
overbought = sell" and "under 30 = oversold = buy", and then bleed money to it for months.

**In a strong trend, an oscillator can stay pinned at its extreme for a very long time.** A market in
a powerful uptrend will push RSI above 70 and *keep it there* for days or weeks, printing new highs
the whole way. Every bar that "should" have been the reversal was just another leg up. Overbought
does not mean expensive and it does not mean sell — it means momentum has been strongly one-sided,
which in a trend is exactly what you would expect, and is often a sign of *strength*, not exhaustion.

:::note{type=warning}
"RSI is over 70, so I should sell (or short)" is not a trade — it is a way to fight a trend and get
run over. The same trap works in reverse: shorting into a strong downtrend because "RSI is oversold"
just hands your stop to the trend. An extreme reading tells you the move is one-sided; it never tells
you the move is about to end.
:::

## So what do you actually do with them?

- Use RSI to describe **context**, not to trigger entries. Overbought inside a range is meaningful;
  overbought in a rip-roaring trend is normal.
- Watch the MACD **histogram for shifts in momentum** — a shrinking histogram warns that a move is
  decelerating before price turns. That is a clue, not a command.
- Treat crosses and band-crossings as *confirmation for a thesis you already hold* from structure and
  levels, never as standalone signals. An oscillator that agrees with a level you were already
  watching is worth far more than one shouting in open space.
