# What crypto actually is

Before you ever open a trade, you need a clear, un-mystical picture of what you are trading. Crypto
is not magic internet money and it is not a scam by definition — it is a set of technologies with
specific properties, some genuinely useful and some wildly oversold.

## A blockchain, at user level

A **blockchain** is a shared ledger: a list of transactions that many independent computers keep a
copy of and agree on. You do not need to understand the cryptography to trade, but you do need three
ideas:

- **No central operator.** There is no company whose database "is" Bitcoin. Thousands of nodes hold
  the same history and reject anything that breaks the rules.
- **Settlement is final.** Once a transaction is confirmed and buried under enough later blocks,
  reversing it is, in practice, impossible. There is no chargeback and no support desk.
- **You hold keys, not accounts.** Ownership is proven by a private key. Whoever controls the key
  controls the coins — which is the whole point, and also the whole danger.

:::note{type=warning}
"Not your keys, not your coins." If your coins sit on an exchange, you own an IOU from that exchange,
not the coins themselves. That distinction becomes very real the day an exchange freezes withdrawals.
:::

## Bitcoin vs altcoins vs stablecoins

These three words get thrown around as if they were the same asset class. They are not.

- **Bitcoin (BTC)** is the oldest and most decentralised chain, designed to be scarce (a fixed supply
  schedule capped at 21 million) and hard to change. Most people treat it as the reserve asset of the
  space — the thing other coins are quoted and measured against.
- **Altcoins** is everything else: from serious platforms with real usage to tokens that exist only
  to be sold to you. "It's an altcoin" tells you nothing about quality — you still have to look.
- **Stablecoins** (USDT, USDC, …) are tokens that aim to hold a fixed value, usually 1 USD, by being
  backed by reserves. They are the cash leg of crypto trading: you park value in them between trades.
  Their stability depends entirely on the issuer actually holding what they claim.

## What confers value, and what doesn't

Value in crypto comes from the same boring places it comes from anywhere: **demand for a real use,
credible scarcity, and network effects** (a chain everyone already builds on is hard to displace).

What does *not* confer durable value: a slick website, a large "market cap" that is really a tiny
float multiplied by a fantasy price, an influencer's endorsement, or the mere fact that a price went
up last week. A token can moon on pure narrative and round-trip all the way back — narrative is a
real force in the short term and worthless collateral in the long term.

:::note{type=info}
Cross-cutting stance for this whole course: indicators, narratives and "signals" are **tools with
known mechanics**, never oracles. We will always show you what a thing actually measures and when it
lies.
:::
