# Fibonacci retracements

After a strong move, price rarely runs in a straight line — it pushes, pulls back, then (often)
continues. **Fibonacci retracements** are a set of horizontal levels traders draw across that move to
estimate *how deep the pullback will go* before the trend resumes. They are a measuring tool, not a
crystal ball.

## The levels

You draw the tool across one clean **impulse leg** — a strong swing from a low to a high, or a high
to a low — and it slices that range into ratios. Three matter for a near-beginner:

- **0.382** — a shallow pullback. The trend barely paused.
- **0.5** — the midpoint. Price gave back half the move.
- **0.618** — the deep "golden" pullback, the one most traders watch.

A "0.618 retracement" means price retraced 61.8% of the impulse before turning. Beyond the pullback
range are **extensions** — 1.272 and 1.618 are the common ones — which project *where the next leg
might reach* if the trend pushes past the old extreme.

:::note{type=info}
Honest footnote on the numbers: 0.618 is the inverse of the golden ratio, and 0.382 is 1 − 0.618, so
those two really do come from the Fibonacci sequence. **0.5 does not** — it is just the halfway
point, kept on the tool because markets respect round halves, not because of any Fibonacci maths.
:::

## How to draw them

Pick one obvious swing — nothing subtle — and anchor the tool to its two ends:

- **In an uptrend:** anchor from the **swing low** (the start of the move) to the **swing high** (the
  end). The retracement levels then fall *below* the high, measuring how far a pullback drops back
  toward the low.
- **In a downtrend:** anchor from the **swing high** to the **swing low**. The levels fall *above*
  the low, measuring how far a bounce climbs back up.

The direction matters: always drag from the start of the impulse to its end. Get that backwards and
your 0.382 and 0.618 swap places.

## The honest why

Here is the part most tutorials skip. There is **no mechanism** by which a market is forced to
respect 61.8% of a move. Prices are set by orders, not by a spiral in a sunflower. So why do these
levels do anything at all?

- **They are self-fulfilling.** A huge number of traders — and the algos built to hunt their orders —
  watch the same 0.382 / 0.5 / 0.618 lines. When enough people rest buy orders near 0.618, buying
  near 0.618 becomes, briefly, a real reason price bounces there. The level works *because* it is
  watched.
- **They add confluence.** A fib level sitting on top of a prior support, a round number, or a moving
  average is worth far more than one hanging in empty space. The fib did not create the level; it
  agreed with one that already mattered.

:::note{type=warning}
The trap to kill now: **price does not have to reverse exactly at 0.618.** It is a *zone*, not a
laser line. Price routinely overshoots, wicks through, and reverses — or stops short before it. If
you place a stop one tick beyond 0.618 expecting a perfect turn, normal noise will stop you out
constantly. Read the level as "a reversal is more likely around here," and let price confirm it.
:::

## What to actually do

1. Find one clean impulse leg and anchor the tool start-to-end.
2. Watch the **0.5–0.618 zone** — that is where most trend pullbacks find a floor.
3. Ask what else lines up there. A fib alone is a guess; a fib on old support with a real reaction is
   a setup.
4. Wait for price to *react* at the level. The level is an alert, not a trigger.
